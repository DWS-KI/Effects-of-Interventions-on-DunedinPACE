# Supplementary Excel table titles and captions

These are the original manuscript titles and captions for the tables exported to Excel.
Keep the stable labels below; Quarto resolves their numbers in Manuscript.qmd.
Inline R expressions in captions are evaluated in the manuscript environment during rendering.

## Supplementary @supptbl-excluded-characteristics: Characteristics of Complete Case vs. Excluded Individuals

**Characteristics of the complete case population versus individuals excluded due to missing covariate data.** BMI, body mass index; IQR, interquartile range; PhD, Doctor of Philosophy; MD, Doctor of Medicine; DO, Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine; DDS, Doctor of Dental Surgery; JD, Juris Doctor; SMD, standardized mean difference. The table compares baseline characteristics between the final complete case population (n = `r format(n_complete_case, big.mark = ",")`) and the `r format(n_excluded_missing_covariate, big.mark = ",")` (`r sprintf("%.1f", pct_excluded_missing_covariate)`%) individuals who satisfied all preceding eligibility criteria but were excluded at the final step because of missing data in at least one remaining primary-model covariate. Participants with missing chronological age or biological sex had already been excluded earlier and were not included in this comparison group. Values are presented to evaluate potential selection bias from complete case analysis. An absolute SMD greater than 0.1 was considered indicative of meaningful imbalance. Positive SMDs indicate higher values for continuous covariates or higher proportions in the listed categories in the excluded group compared with the complete case population; negative SMDs indicate lower values for continuous covariates or lower proportions in the listed categories in the excluded group compared with the complete case population.

## Supplementary @supptbl-training-results: Effects of Exercise on DunedinPACE

**Effects of exercise frequency on DunedinPACE.** ATE, average treatment effect; CI, confidence interval; G-comp, G-computation; IPW, inverse probability weighting; SMD, standardized mean difference. The table presents the estimated effects of exercising 1–2, 3–4, 5–7, and 8+ times per week compared with never exercising on DunedinPACE. Estimates are shown from both IPW and G-computation analyses together with 95% confidence intervals. The Residual imbalance column lists covariates or categories with post-IPW \|SMD\| > 0.1; parentheses contain signed SMDs rounded to two decimals. All imbalanced levels of categorical covariates are listed. Positive SMDs indicate higher values for continuous covariates or higher proportions in the listed categories among participants in the indicated exercise-frequency group compared with those who never exercise after IPW; negative SMDs indicate lower values for continuous covariates or lower proportions in the listed categories among participants in the indicated exercise-frequency group compared with those who never exercise after IPW. “None” indicates that no covariate exceeds the cutoff. ATE percentages and their 95% confidence limits express absolute DunedinPACE differences as percentages of the reference pace of 1 biological year per calendar year. Divide the displayed ATE percentage and each confidence limit by 100, retaining their signs, to recover absolute differences in DunedinPACE units.

## Supplementary @supptbl-extreme-weights: Extreme Weights Sensitivity Analysis

**Extreme weights sensitivity analysis.** ATE, average treatment effect; CI, confidence interval; IPW, inverse probability weighting. The table presents the estimated intervention effects on DunedinPACE using standard IPW weights (method used in the rest of the study) alongside a distinct method for handling extreme weights: truncated weights. Truncation at the 1st and 99th percentiles provides a hard cap to prevent rare individual influence. For each intervention, numbers of users and non-users and ATEs with 95% confidence intervals for both methods are shown. Comparison with the main untruncated IPW estimates assesses sensitivity to extreme weights; truncation does not establish robustness to positivity violations. ATE percentages and their 95% confidence limits express absolute DunedinPACE differences as percentages of the reference pace of 1 biological year per calendar year. Divide the displayed ATE percentage and each confidence limit by 100, retaining their signs, to recover absolute differences in DunedinPACE units.

## Supplementary @supptbl-sensitivity-combinations: Exact Intervention Combinations and Their Constituent Interventions

**Exact intervention combinations and their constituent interventions: an exploratory sensitivity analysis.** ATE, average treatment effect; CI, confidence interval; G-comp, G-computation; IPW, inverse probability weighting; SMD, standardized mean difference. The table presents the `r n_top_combinations` most frequent exact-use combinations of at least two interventions and repeats the main-analysis estimates only for the `r n_component_interventions` individual interventions present in those combinations. Exact-combination membership requires all listed interventions and none of the remaining evaluated interventions; N Exposed gives the intersection count and N Reference counts all other participants. Individual intervention rows compare users with non-users of the named intervention, regardless of other intervention use. Exact-combination rows compare users of the exact listed combination with all other participants. The purpose is to explore whether common combinations show larger DunedinPACE decreases than their constituent intervention exposures; comparisons are descriptive and do not test additive or synergistic effects or directly compare against exclusive single-intervention users. Both methods retain the main cohort, nine primary covariates, and bootstrap procedures, with no adjustment for other interventions or combinations. Rows follow the figure's signed IPW estimate order, from most negative to most positive. The table reports both methods' estimates, 95% confidence intervals, and group counts. Residual imbalance lists covariates or categories with post-IPW \|SMD\| > 0.1 and their signed SMDs rounded to two decimals; all imbalanced categorical levels are listed. Positive values indicate higher covariate values or category proportions in the exposed group after weighting, and negative values indicate lower values or proportions, relative to that row's reference group. “None” means no covariate exceeds the threshold. ATE percentages and their confidence limits express absolute DunedinPACE differences as percentages of the reference pace of 1 biological year per calendar year; divide each by 100, retaining its sign, to recover absolute DunedinPACE differences.
